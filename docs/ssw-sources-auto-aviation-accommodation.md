# SSW curricula: automotive maintenance, aviation, and accommodation

**Sources checked:** official Japanese primary sources, accessed 2026-10-22.  
**Content policy:** the curricula use original Indonesian explanations and newly written Japanese examples. They summarize work and test scope; they do not reproduce official exam questions or imply that the in-app exercises are official test material.

## Automotive maintenance (自動車整備)

The Immigration Services Agency (ISA) describes SSW No. 1 work as daily and periodic vehicle inspection and maintenance, specified maintenance, and basic associated tasks. SSW No. 2 covers the same types of work at a general level while guiding other personnel. The ISA lists the SSW No. 1 skill route as the field evaluation test or Automotive Mechanic Skills Proficiency Test Grade 3; the required Japanese route is JFT-Basic or JLPT N4 or higher. For No. 2, the listed skill routes are the field evaluation test or Mechanic Grade 2, with practical experience at a certified maintenance shop. [ISA automotive maintenance sector](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/policies/ssw/automobilerepair.html)

The Japan Automobile Service Promotion Association (JASPA) is the evaluation test body. Its current exam guidance describes a chassis-and-engine scope. No. 1 covers basic structure and operation, inspection/repair/adjustment, tools and measuring instruments, materials and fluids, and basic practical work. JASPA describes the No. 1 exam as a 30-question true/false written test (60 minutes) and three practical judgment tasks with multiple prompts (20 minutes). The No. 2 written test is 40 multiple-choice questions (80 minutes); the practical judgment test has three tasks (30 minutes). The No. 2 exam applicant must have at least three years of maintenance experience at a certified shop before the exam date. [JASPA evaluation test guidance](https://www.jaspa.or.jp/mechanic/specific-skill/index.html) [MLIT automotive SSW page](https://www.mlit.go.jp/jidosha/jidosha_SSW.html)

A Japanese mechanic examination is an alternate skill-standard route listed by ISA; the curricula do not present a Japanese mechanic certificate as a prerequisite for every SSW worker. The ISA skill-standard page does not list an ordinary driver's licence as a general SSW eligibility item. That does not authorize a worker to drive a customer vehicle: any road test or vehicle movement must be assigned to a person with the appropriate driving licence and workplace authorization. A receiving business must also meet the sector-specific certified-shop conditions. [ISA automotive maintenance sector](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/policies/ssw/automobilerepair.html)

## Aviation (航空)

ISA identifies two work divisions: airport ground handling and aircraft maintenance. Its No. 1 language threshold is JFT-Basic at the stated A2.2-equivalent level or JLPT N4 or higher. No. 2 ground handling includes managing work processes as a qualified instructor or team leader; No. 2 aircraft maintenance includes carrying out specialist technical maintenance based on one's own judgment within the defined scope. [ISA aviation sector](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/policies/ssw/aviation.html) [ISA No. 2 job descriptions](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/policies/ssw/10_00180.html)

**Current ground-handling scope:** MLIT's current examination guideline took effect on 2026-08-01. JAEA states that this revision added passenger handling, transport/loading/unloading of in-flight meals and related items, and aviation-fuel handling to the four already covered work areas: aircraft ground-movement support, baggage/cargo handling, baggage/cargo loading and unloading, and aircraft interior/exterior cleaning. The lessons therefore cover the updated seven activity areas, while treating fuel operations and other hazardous work as authorized specialist tasks. The ground-handling No. 2 exam application requires a Skills Progress Confirmation Sheet. [MLIT aviation SSW page and exam guidelines](https://www.mlit.go.jp/koku/koku_fr19_000011.html) [MLIT ground-handling guideline, effective 2026-08-01](https://www.mlit.go.jp/koku/content/002001685.pdf) [JAEA 2026 revision notice and updated study texts](https://exam.jaea.or.jp/?page_id=51378)

For aircraft maintenance, JAEA's study materials identify three subject groups: basic aircraft skills (fastening and electrical measurement), work safety and maintenance quality, and aircraft overview. The No. 2 exam is run by JAEA and uses a Japanese written paper of about 35 multiple-choice questions in 90 minutes plus a practical task test for fastening and mechanical measurement. Applicants need at least three years of relevant specialist aircraft-maintenance experience; an eligible foreign aviation-maintenance credential may exempt the practical test under the published rules. The No. 1 examination guideline and JAEA study page describe written and practical assessment in the basic subject groups. [JAEA aircraft-maintenance study materials](https://exam.jaea.or.jp/?page_id=389) [JAEA No. 2 exam guide](https://exam.jaea.or.jp/?page_id=30043) [MLIT No. 2 exam guideline](https://www.mlit.go.jp/koku/content/001872766.pdf)

## Accommodation (宿泊)

ISA defines SSW No. 1 accommodation work as providing lodging services such as front desk, planning/public relations, guest service, and restaurant service. No. 2 includes providing those services while guiding multiple employees. ISA lists the No. 1 skill test and JFT-Basic or JLPT N4 or higher; it describes No. 2 as requiring the No. 2 skill test and supervised-service experience. [ISA accommodation sector](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/policies/ssw/accommodation.html)

The Accommodation Industry Skills Test Center (CAIPT) identifies five test categories: front-desk work, planning/public relations, customer service, restaurant service, and safety/hygiene plus basic knowledge. Its current page describes No. 1 as 30 written and six practical questions, each with three choices; No. 2 as 50 written and 20 practical questions, each with four choices. CAIPT states that a No. 2 test application requires proof of at least two years of accommodation work experience. This is the test body's application requirement; the ISA's description also requires No. 2 work to include supervising multiple employees while providing lodging services. [CAIPT exam scope and application information](https://caipt.or.jp/tokuteiginou) [Japan Tourism Agency accommodation SSW page and official guidelines](https://www.mlit.go.jp/kankocho/seisaku_seido/kihonkeikaku/jizoku_kankochi/kankojinzai/ninaitekakuho/tokutei_gino.html)

## Gaps and limits

- The lessons are original study material in Indonesian with short Japanese workplace phrases. Their three-choice-independent app checks are not the official test format; every app check has four options for consistent learning practice.
- The lessons teach common safety decisions and communication. They do not replace a workplace's current manuals, maintenance data, airport rules, hotel procedures, authorized qualifications, equipment training, or supervisor instructions.
- The automotive lessons do not supply model-specific values, diagnose real vehicles, or authorize work on high-voltage systems. Driving and road tests remain subject to the correct licence and local authorization.
- The aviation lessons summarize both divisions but cannot cover every aircraft type, airport, company procedure, dangerous-goods class, aircraft loading calculation, or fuelling method. They deliberately do not train users to operate restricted equipment or perform specialist work without authorization.
- Accommodation lessons do not decide immigration eligibility, facility licensing, pricing, food-allergen safety, or emergency-response requirements for a specific property. Those decisions must use current official and employer procedures.
- Official pages can change. Applicants should check current exam dates, eligibility, exemptions, application documents, and residence-status requirements directly with ISA, MLIT, JASPA, JAEA, or CAIPT before applying.
