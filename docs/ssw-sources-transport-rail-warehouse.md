# SSW curricula: road transport, railway, and logistics warehouse

**Access date:** 2026-10-22

This note records the Japanese government and official test-body pages checked while preparing the three original study curricula. The lessons are Indonesian-language workplace learning material with beginner Japanese vocabulary and dialogues. Their examples and three lesson checks were written for this app; they are not official test questions, a test blueprint, a licensing course, or a replacement for employer procedures, required training, or supervision.

## Road transport

### Verified work scope and entry information

The Immigration Services Agency’s [automobile transport sector page](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/policies/ssw/automobiletransportation.html) and its [sector job descriptions](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/ssw/10_00179.html) distinguish three work categories: truck driver, bus driver, and taxi driver. The scope includes commercial vehicle operation and related work. MLIT’s current [automobile transport SSW information page](https://www.mlit.go.jp/jidosha/jidosha_tk1_000038.html) links the current operational documents and the Japan Maritime Association test portal. The [operational guidance published on the MLIT page](https://www.mlit.go.jp/jidosha/content/001977550.pdf) clarifies the work distinction: truck work combines safe freight transport and cargo handling; bus and taxi work combine safe passenger transport and customer service. Associated work such as vehicle cleaning may be done only incidentally where permitted by the rules.

The current ISA page describes different requirements by category. Truck applicants need the truck skill evaluation and a Japanese first-class driver’s license, plus JFT-Basic or JLPT N4 or above. Bus and taxi applicants need the category skill evaluation and a Japanese second-class driver’s license, plus JLPT N3 or above; the ISA page also requires completion of new-driver training for bus and taxi. Details can change, so applicants should check the current ISA and MLIT pages before applying.

### Test information

MLIT identifies the Japan Maritime Association as the test administrator and links its [automobile transport skill-test portal](https://sswt-portal.classnk.or.jp/). MLIT announced the start of the first SSW No. 1 skill tests in December 2024 and directs candidates to the administrator for application details in its [test-start announcement](https://www.mlit.go.jp/report/press/jidosha01_hh_000097.html). The published [test implementation guidance](https://www.mlit.go.jp/jidosha/content/001851696.pdf) describes a Japanese-language category test and the official administration methods. Schedules, venues, and current registration availability are maintained by the administrator and can change.

The lessons therefore distinguish freight handling and delivery from bus/taxi passenger service. They teach work communication and safe escalation at a beginner language level; they do not claim to cover driving skills, the full legal scope, official test content, or the higher Japanese requirement for passenger-driver categories.

## Railway

### Verified work scope

MLIT’s current [railway foreign-worker information page](https://www.mlit.go.jp/tetudo/tetudo_fr7_000056.html) and the [January 2026 sector policy](https://www.mlit.go.jp/tetudo/content/001978642.pdf) identify six work categories: track maintenance, electrical-facility maintenance, rolling-stock maintenance, rolling-stock manufacturing, transport staff, and station/rolling-stock cleaning. The linked [official descriptions of railway work](https://www.mlit.go.jp/tetudo/content/001769018.pdf) give examples including track measurement and repair; power, signal, crossing, and communication equipment; vehicle inspection and repair; parts receipt and distribution; train movement, station equipment, passenger and freight handling, and train operation; and cleaning stations and vehicles.

The January 2026 policy specifies Japanese-language benchmarks by category: A2.2 or above for track, electrical, rolling-stock maintenance, manufacturing, and station/vehicle cleaning; B1 or above for transport staff. These policy benchmarks are higher than the A1–A2 Japanese used in the app lessons. This curriculum is introductory workplace language practice and must not be treated as proof that a learner meets an immigration or work qualification.

### Test information and limits

MLIT’s railway page links the skill-test implementation guidance and test administrators for five categories: track maintenance, electrical-facility maintenance, rolling-stock maintenance, rolling-stock manufacturing, and transport staff. The administrator links include the [Japan Railway Civil Engineering Association](https://www.jrcea.or.jp/tokuteiginou) for track work, the [Railway Electrical Engineering Safety Association](https://www.railecr.com/news/20241101.html) for electrical work, the [Japan Railway Rolling Stock Machinery Association](https://www.rma.or.jp/ssw/index.html) for rolling-stock maintenance, the [Japan Railway Rolling Stock Industries Association](https://www.tetsushako.or.jp/specific_skills.html) for manufacturing, and the [Japan Train Operators Association](https://www.jtoa.or.jp/ssw/index.html) for transport staff. Current registration dates and venues must be checked on each administrator’s page.

Station/rolling-stock cleaning appears as a sixth work and skill-evaluation category in the January 2026 policy, but the MLIT railway page’s linked test-guidance and administrator list checked for this note still shows the five categories above. I did not find a published cleaning test schedule or current implementation details on that page on the access date, so this note does not assert that the new category’s test is available or specify its exam format.

The lessons cover safety communication across public-facing work, movement coordination, track/electrical maintenance, vehicles and parts, manufacturing traceability, and cleaning. Operating railway equipment, entering a railway work area, performing technical maintenance, or returning an asset to service requires the employer’s authorization, training, and site procedures. The app content cannot replace them.

## Logistics warehouse

### Verified work scope and entry information

MLIT’s current [logistics-warehouse SSW page](https://www.mlit.go.jp/seisakutokatsu/freight/warehousing_ssw.html) limits the main work to warehouse tasks inside a logistics warehouse. It specifically lists handover and inspection of inbound/outbound cargo, movement, put-away, picking, and distribution processing. The page describes related duties such as reporting to the office, organizing and cleaning work areas, and moving goods or preventing rainwater entry in preparation for a typhoon. Its FAQ says distribution processing can include small-lot repacking, attaching tags, labeling, and kitting; it does not include manufacturing that completes or improves the product’s function. The page also describes use of a system with inbound, inventory, and outbound functions (a warehouse-management system).

For SSW No. 1, MLIT lists a sector skill-evaluation test and Japanese at A2 or above, such as JLPT N4. The page says no SSW No. 2 intake is currently provided for this sector.

### Test information and explicit gap

On the MLIT page as checked on 2026-10-22, the sector-specific test implementation guidance was still described as under adjustment and to be published later. The page says trial tests for both the SSW No. 1 skill evaluation and the initial Training and Employment skill evaluation had been completed by the end of February 2026 and that the approval process was under way. It states that the SSW No. 1 test start date was still undecided; the initial Training and Employment test is expected after that system begins in April 2027. The page also says the sector council was still being organized. These are the official page’s stated statuses; they are not an inference that an exam can be booked. Check MLIT for later notices.

Because the official test details and start date are pending on the cited page, the warehouse curriculum teaches the published work scope only. It does not state an official exam format, question count, test schedule, test body, or sample questions. Its receive–store–pick–pack–ship and traceability scenarios are original learning practice based on the official task categories and general warehouse workflow.
